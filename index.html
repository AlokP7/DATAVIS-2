The Changing Cost
of Flying in Australia

Fifteen years of BITRE data traces how Australian domestic airfares have shifted — from a long decade of falling real prices, through the COVID collapse, to an uncertain recovery. Are we flying cheaper than ever? The answer is more complicated than it looks.

Australia's Busiest Air Routes

The Melbourne–Sydney corridor is the world's third-busiest air route, carrying over 8 million passengers in 2024. The east-coast triangle — Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane — dominates domestic aviation, while long-haul routes to Perth and Cairns connect remote Australia to the major hubs. This is the network whose fares this dashboard explores.

02

Where Is Flying Most Expensive?

Before looking at how fares have changed, it helps to understand the geographic landscape of cost. Travellers departing from Western Australia and the Northern Territory face average real fares nearly double those from Victoria or Tasmania — a direct consequence of vast distances, thin competition, and the economics of serving remote Australia.

Why has Canberra risen so sharply? The collapse of Rex Airlines in 2024 was particularly felt on Canberra routes. With Rex gone and Virgin and Qantas as the only remaining carriers, reduced competition has pushed average fares above $400 — the highest on record for CBR.
03

Are Fares Getting Cheaper?

Adjusted for inflation, domestic fares fell steadily through the 2010s as low-cost carriers expanded. COVID reversed that entirely — real fares on many routes more than doubled. Most have since recovered, but in real terms many routes are more expensive in 2024 than in 2010 — driven by the collapse of Rex Airlines and Bonza in 2024.

Which Routes Have Changed Most?

The trend story plays out very differently route by route. Short regional hops are consistently the worst value per kilometre, while long-haul routes benefit from economies of scale and stronger competition. And when you compare 2010 to 2024, the winners and losers are not always who you'd expect.

05

When Is the Cheapest Time to Fly?

Knowing that fares vary by route and by geography, the next question for any traveller is when to fly. Domestic fares follow clear seasonal rhythms. February is consistently the cheapest month on the Melbourne–Sydney route. April and December school holidays push prices higher. COVID shattered this pattern in 2020 — prices spiked even as passenger numbers collapsed.

Monthly real fare heatmap — Melbourne–Sydney, 2015–2026
Each cell = average real fare for that month and year. Darker blue = more expensive. Read across a row for the seasonal pattern within one year; read down a column to compare the same month across years.
Seasonal fare profile by departure city, 2024
Each panel = average monthly fare from that city across all routes in 2024. The seasonal shape is consistent across cities; the absolute level varies widely. Perth departures (~$400) cost roughly double Sydney departures (~$200).
06

How Busy Are Australia's Skies?

Fares don't exist in isolation — they reflect the balance between seat supply and passenger demand. The Melbourne–Sydney corridor has grown almost continuously since deregulation in the 1980s, until COVID brought it to a near-complete halt. The recovery was fast: by 2023 passenger numbers were back above pre-COVID levels, and load factors suggest airlines are running full planes at higher prices.
